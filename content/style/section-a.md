# Section A style notes (paraphrased; no question text)

Caveat: most of these notes come from text extraction, so the 2023 reasoning and arithmetic stems (stored as images) were not profiled. A sample of the June 2023 pages was read by eye, including the Hindi section.

## General Awareness
- 20 questions, one-line stems, four options, almost all single-fact recall. Many are "which of these is correct" with no reasoning needed.
- Aug 2021 papers lean on the two-statement format (I/II, then Only I / Only II / Both / Neither): about 10 of 20 in tgtcs4 and about 8 of 20 in tgtcs2. The 2023 and 2024 papers mostly dropped it.
- Constitution items ask for the article or schedule number for a topic (language, services commission, liberty, acting president, borrowing, amendment procedure) or a simple constitutional fact (Panchayat age, Rajya Sabha chair).
- Broad coverage every time: history, geography, polity, economics, everyday science, culture, sports. Each paper has about 3 science, 3 geography, 3 history and 2 culture items. Everyday science is thin in 2024 (2) and 2023 (1).
- Economics stays basic: money supply, intermediate goods, repo-type rate terms, a bank founding year. Note-size and note-back-image questions appeared in both 2021 papers.
- Culture items cover festivals/fairs by state, dance-to-state pairs, dance technique terms, a classical-epic author, Carnatic trinity, UNESCO sites. Expect a state-matching flavour.
- Sports and awards items: Olympic medal counts, World Cup / badminton championship results, Ranji final, football awards.
- Current affairs: about 6 of 20 in 2024 (Dec 2023 repo rate, Dec 2023 AI project, Nov 2023 MoU, a 2023 world championship, an ISRO MoU of Jan 2023), so mostly 1 to 14 months before the 7 Feb 2024 exam.
- 2023 paper has about 11 current-type items drawn from 2020-2022 (a 2022 World Cup award, a 2022 domestic final, a 2021 fund, an annual report of 2021-22, an awards count for 2021), roughly 6 to 24 months old for the June 2023 exam.
- Aug 2021 papers: only 3-4 current items, all from the same year (Padma awards 2021, Oscar 2021, a 2021 report), plus older ones (2016 Olympics, 2019 U20 football), so 1 to 8 months old, and some are years old.
- Delhi-specific content: essentially none. Delhi shows up only as a monument location, a distractor team name, and a High Court mentioned in an English passage. No Delhi government, MCD or Delhi history topic in any of the four.
- Obscure counts are used as hard items (number of award winners, note dimensions). Roughly 4 to 6 hard per paper, 4 to 12 easy.

## Reasoning
- Mix of verbal and numeric/letter work plus a small non-verbal block. Verbal-only share is high in 2024: no figure-based items were evident except one data-style item.
- Coding-decoding appears 1-2 times, as letter-value sums mapped to a number or reversed-block rearrangement; moderate effort.
- Series appear 2-3 times: letter series with a missing term, "one wrong term" letter series (the Aug 2021 favourite, hard), and number series with alternating operations.
- Analogy and classification appear in letter, word and number forms; number analogies treat the number as a whole and forbid digit splitting.
- Logic block: syllogism with 2-3 statements and 2-3 conclusions appears every paper. 2024 also adds statement-argument strength, statement-conclusion, and statement-courses of action as separate items; these are easy.
- Arrangement: one linear or circular seating puzzle (7-8 people) per paper, medium to hard; Aug 2021 adds an assignment-style puzzle with professions and transport, and a team-selection puzzle.
- Direction sense: multi-leg walks with right or anticlockwise turns, ending with distance and direction from the start (2 in each Aug 2021 paper, none in 2024).
- Mathematical operations: symbol substitution then evaluation, or "swap two numbers and test equations"; one 2021 item was annulled for a discrepancy.
- Venn/data counting appears 2 times in 2021 (diagram counts and statement checks) and once in 2024.
- Non-verbal block of 3 items in 2021 (odd figure, paper folding, mirror image, cube painting). In 2024 this block seems absent.
- Blood relations: coded relation symbols, 1 per paper at most.
- Difficulty shape: mostly medium (11-12 of 20); hard 4-7, with letter-series, circular seating, long walks and cube painting as the hard items.

## Arithmetic
- Word problems dominate, at 2 to 3 percent-style items, 2 interest items, 2 mensuration items, 2 speed-distance items, 1 work item per paper.
- Chart block of 3 items every paper: a pie chart item with a total given, a bar or line chart with I/II/III statements to verify, or a computed expression over chart values. These are image-based.
- Number-system items are small: divisibility with a missing digit, primes, LCM/HCF real-world (bells), HCF of three numbers.
- Interest: simple interest with changing rates, SI vs CI over 2 years to find the principal (hard), and standard CI over 3 years.
- Mensuration: rectangle from ratio plus area, percentage change in cuboid surface, rhombus from sum of diagonals, melting a hemisphere into a cone.
- Trains and speed appear in every paper: platform crossing, opposite-direction crossing, meeting time, average speed.
- Simplification is a single BODMAS line; in 2024 one such item and no decimals item.
- Typical wording is compact (1-3 lines) with options in the same units; answers are mostly integers or terminating decimals, the percentage ones sometimes recurring (16.66, 14.28).
- Difficulty: 3-4 hard (chart-statement and train items), 6-8 easy.
- No data-interpretation table or graph in 2024 other than a line chart and a pie chart.

## English
- Always 20 items; the final 5 (Q16-20 in 2024 and 2023, Q96-100 in the Aug 2021 papers) are one passage.
- Passage length: about 130 words (2024, with a statistics-heavy paragraph), about 230 words (tgtcs4, two paragraphs), about 250 words (tgtcs2), about 430 words (2023, five paragraphs). Longer passages were in the older and the mid papers; 2024 is the shortest.
- Passage questions: main idea or title, detail retrieval incl. one "NOT mentioned" or "which does not include", implication of a line, vocabulary-in-context synonym or antonym, and in one paper a purpose-of-opening question.
- Vocabulary: one synonym, one or two antonyms, usually with common words (some are rare, e.g. a few Latinate adjectives).
- Spelling: 2 per paper, as "pick the correct sentence/word" or "pick the wrong word".
- Idioms: 2 per paper, with a situation-to-idiom form in 2024 (choose the idiom for a situation) and meaning-of-idiom form in the other three.
- Fill in the blanks: prepositions, phrasal verbs, question tags, adverb vs adjective, tense (past perfect), vocabulary choice. 2024 has 4-5 such items, with two question-tag items.
- Error spotting in sentence parts ("No error" as 4th option) in 2021 and 2023; absent in 2024.
- Sentence improvement (underlined segment, "No improvement" option) in 2021 and 2023; absent in 2024.
- Rearrangement: parts P/Q/R/S of one sentence (2021, 2024 once) and full-paragraph jumbles of 4 sentences (2023, 2024 once) - these are the hardest items.
- One-word substitution: 1-2 per paper (2021 and 2023); absent in 2024. Words are mid-frequency ("-cracy" words in one paper).
- Difficulty: 7-10 easy, 8-11 medium, 1-5 hard.

## Hindi
- 20 items, with the last 5 always a gadyansh passage with sub-questions; the earlier 15 are language items.
- Passage topics per extract: a short piece on a historical-political episode (the Aug 2021 papers), with about 5 questions on meaning, inference and detail.
- 2024 has two sentence-ordering items (options as number sequences like 1-2-4-3) and two fill-in-the-blank items, which is the exam's new pattern.
- Items whose options are A+B pairs appear 1-2 times per paper: sandhi vichchhed or samas vigraha style.
- Punctuation-mark choice (viram chinh) appeared in both Aug 2021 papers (1-2 items).
- A June 2023 shift, read by eye, used these shapes: one word for a phrase, a proverb or idiom with one wrong word to correct, choose the correct sentence, sandhi-vichchhed, vilom, fill in the blank, synonym for an underlined word in a sentence, a blank to fill with a word of a named samas, and tadbhav of an underlined word. The last five questions were a passage, including a choose-the-title question.
- Text extraction loses Devanagari, so there is no exact per-type count across papers. Only that one shift was read in full detail.
